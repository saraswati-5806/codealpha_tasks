Lesson 06

Real-World Examples

Real cases show how phishing can spread quickly and why users must verify links, emails, and requests.

2016 — Google Docs Phishing Worm

What happened: Users received fake Google Docs invitations.

How it worked: The attacker abused trust in shared document links.

Prevention: Verify sender, permissions, and app access requests.

COVID-19 WHO Phishing Emails

What happened: Fake health alerts pretended to be from WHO.

How it worked: Fear and urgency pushed users to click malicious links.

Prevention: Use official websites for health or government updates.

2020 — Twitter Bitcoin Scam

What happened: High-profile accounts posted cryptocurrency scam messages.

How it worked: Internal employees were socially engineered.

Prevention: Strong access control, training, and verification steps.

Corporate Spear Phishing Scenario

What happened: A fake CEO email requests urgent wire transfer.

How it worked: Authority and urgency pressured an employee.

Prevention: Confirm payment requests through a second channel.
